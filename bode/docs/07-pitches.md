# BODE Pitches

## Elevator pitch (30 seconds)

> Every summer, millions of students walk across campuses that hit 105°F, with no shade and nowhere to hide. **BODE is shade that follows you.** Open the app, unlock a BODE, and a solar-powered drone flies to you and holds the exact angle between you and the sun the whole way to class. Its canopy is a solar panel, so the angle that shades you is the angle that charges it. That gets us four times the flight time of a normal drone. It works like Lime: $1 to unlock, 25 cents a minute. We're launching on three Sun Belt campuses next fall, and we're raising a seed round to build the first 180 drones.

**10-second version:** "BODE is Lime for shade: solar drones that follow you across campus and keep you out of the sun."

## Shark Tank pitch (about 2 minutes, then Q&A)

**The ask:** $500,000 for 10% of BODE (a $5M valuation).

### Walk-on

*(Founder walks out under a hovering violet BODE canopy, which keeps them in shade as they cross the stage to their mark. Stage lights stand in for the sun.)*

> Hi Sharks. I'm [Name], founder of BODE, and I'm asking for $500,000 for 10% of my company.
>
> Raise your hand if you've ever walked across a parking lot in July and felt like you were melting. *(beat)* Now imagine that's your commute four times a day, because you're one of the 4 million students at colleges across the Sun Belt.
>
> Umbrellas don't work. You need a free hand, they block your view, and nobody carries one when it's sunny. Trees and awnings stay where they are. You move.
>
> So I built shade that moves. *(gestures up)* This is BODE.

### How it works

> BODE is an AI drone with a one-meter solar canopy. It tracks you with on-device vision and GPS, calculates where the sun is to a hundredth of a degree, and flies to the one spot in the sky that puts its shadow on your head. When you walk, it leads you. When the sun moves, it re-angles.
>
> Here's the magic part. To shade you, the canopy has to face the sun, and that's exactly how you aim a solar panel. **The angle that shades you is the angle that charges it.** At midday that cuts BODE's battery draw by three-quarters. A normal drone this size flies 20 minutes. BODE flies about 80.

### Business model

> We don't sell BODE to consumers. We run it like Lime. Drones sit on solar "Roosts" around campus. You unlock one in the app for a dollar plus 25 cents a minute, or get unlimited rides with a $14.99 Campus Pass. When you're done, it flies itself home. Nothing gets left on the sidewalk.
>
> We also plug into scooter apps through open standards, so a rider can park a scooter and have a BODE meet them for the walk to class. The partner gets a cut.

### Numbers

> Each drone costs about $1,100 to build today, dropping to $700 at volume. In our model a drone earns back its cost in about 115 days in year one and 65 days by year two, at a 57 to 64% contribution margin. We plan 3 campuses next year, 15 the year after, and 45 in year three. That's about $11 million in revenue in year three.
>
> Your $500,000 builds our first 60-drone pilot fleet, gets FAA approvals, and launches on our first campus.
>
> So, Sharks: who wants to get out of the sun?

### Prepared answers to the hard questions

**"Drones over people? Isn't that illegal?"**
> Flights over people are allowed under FAA Part 107 Subpart D when the aircraft meets a risk category. We're designing BODE to Category 3: guarded rotors, low impact energy, and Remote ID built in. We fly only inside campus-approved geofences, and licensed pilots supervise the fleet from an ops center. Our first pilots run on private campus property under university agreements.

**"What happens when it falls on someone?"**
> Rotors sit outside the canopy and are ducted, the rim is foam, and the drone carries a parachute for failures above 3 meters. We carry aviation and product liability insurance from day one, and it's in our cost model at $240 per drone per year.

**"Solar can't keep a drone in the air."**
> You're right, and we don't claim it does. Solar covers about 75% of hover power at midday. The rest comes from the battery, which is why rides last up to 80 minutes instead of 20. The Roosts handle full recharging in the sun between rides.

**"Why won't DJI or Lime just copy you?"**
> DJI sells hardware; they don't run fleets on campuses. Lime has never run aircraft, and aviation operations, FAA approvals, and university contracts are slow to build. We'll have campus exclusivity agreements and riders' habits first. We're also filing a provisional patent on coupling shade geometry with the solar-tracking control loop.

**"What about wind? Rain? Winter?"**
> BODE stays on its Roost above about 20 mph winds and in rain or lightning. That's why we launch in the Sun Belt, where there are 200+ shade-worthy days a year. In off-months, fleets move to events: graduations, football tailgates, and outdoor festivals, sold through BODE for Teams.

**"Isn't it noisy?"**
> We use larger, slower-turning ducted props, and our design target is under 70 decibels at head height, about the level of a normal conversation. Campuses also set no-fly quiet hours around classrooms and dorms. And the downwash feels like a breeze.

**"What are your sales today?"**
> We're pre-revenue. We have a working shade-positioning engine, a rider app, a partner API, and a waitlist that ranks campuses by demand. The next milestone is a 60-drone pilot on one campus, and that's what this money funds.
